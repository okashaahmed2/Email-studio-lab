**Status:** Draft plan. No emails written or sent yet.
**Written:** 7 Nov 2026
**Campaign dates:** Monday 7 December 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027 (4 weeks)

## 1. Campaign overview

**Campaign name:** "Your Next Step" (working name)

**One-sentence summary:** A 4-week email series that invites our waitlist to join the first cohort of the 12-week Data Science Bootcamp, with honest detail about what the course covers, how much effort it takes, and how mentors support students.

**Main goal:** 150 waitlist members enrol in the first cohort by Sunday 3 January 2027.

That is 7.5% of a 2,000-person list. A waitlist is a warm audience (these people asked to hear from us), but 7.5% is still an ambitious target for a paid course. We have no past campaign results to compare with, so treat this number as a stretch goal, and use the weekly checkpoints in section 7 to see early whether we are on track.

**Secondary goals:**
- Learn which of the three groups responds best, so we can plan future cohorts.
- Collect the most common questions and worries, to improve the course page and future emails.
- Keep the list healthy: low unsubscribes and spam complaints, so future emails still reach inboxes.

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the bootcamp waitlist. All have already shown interest, so they are in the **consideration stage**: they know about us and are deciding whether the bootcamp is right for them. Our job is to answer their questions honestly and make the next step clear, not to persuade them from zero.

Most readers already have intermediate data science skills and want a stronger portfolio.

### Group A: Students (university students and fresh graduates)

**Profile:** A student or recent graduate who has done some data science in class, but has little to show employers outside of coursework. They want real projects for their portfolio.

- **Pain points:** Class projects look the same as everyone else's. Unsure how to use real, messy data. Limited money and time around exams.
- **Motivations:** A portfolio that stands out. Feedback from someone who works in the field.
- **Likely worries:** "Can I fit this around my studies?" "Is it worth the cost?"
- **Timing note:** Many will be on winter break during weeks 3 and 4, which may give them more time to read and decide.

### Group B: Career-switchers (moving into data science from another field)

**Profile:** Someone working in another field who has taught themselves some data science and wants structured training and proof of skill before changing careers.

- **Pain points:** Nervous about coding and statistics. Unsure whether self-study is "enough". No one to check their work.
- **Motivations:** A clear, step by step path. A mentor who reviews their work. Projects they can talk about in interviews.
- **Likely worries:** "Am I too far behind?" "Will this actually get me a job?" (We must answer this honestly: we do not promise jobs. See section 3.)
- **Note:** This group needs the most reassurance and the calmest tone. The brand guide names them as the most nervous readers.

### Group C: Working professionals (adding data skills to their current job)

**Profile:** Someone already in a role such as marketing, finance or operations who wants to use data skills in their current job, not change careers.

- **Pain points:** Limited time. Needs skills they can use at work straight away.
- **Motivations:** Practical skills such as SQL and data visualisation. A fully online course that fits around work.
- **Likely worries:** "How many hours a week does this take?" "Can I keep up with a full-time job?"

### Where they spend time

For this campaign, the only channel is email, which suits a waitlist well: every person on the list asked to hear from us. (If we later add free channels, these groups are also likely to be on LinkedIn, university career groups, and data science communities. That is out of scope for this plan.)

## 3. Key messages

### Core message

> Build a portfolio of 4 real projects in 12 weeks, step by step, with a mentor who reviews your work.

### Supporting messages

| # | Message | Answers the worry | Proof we can state (from the brand guide) |
|---|---------|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **You'll finish with real work to show.** You complete 4 real projects using real public datasets, and leave with a portfolio you can show to employers. | "My portfolio looks like everyone else's." | 4 real projects; real public datasets; portfolio for employers. |
| 2 | **You won't be learning alone.** Every student has a mentor who reviews their projects. | "No one checks my work." / "I'm nervous about coding." | Every student gets a mentor who reviews their projects. |
| 3 | **It moves from basic to advanced, at a steady pace.** We start with a quick Python refresher, then move on to pandas (a Python tool for working with tables of data), SQL (a language for getting data out of databases), data visualisation and machine learning basics. | "Am I too far behind?" / "Will it be too basic for me?" | Published course outline. |
| 4 | **It's honest work, and it fits around your life.** The bootcamp takes real effort. It's 12 weeks and fully online, so you can study from anywhere. | "Can I fit this in?" | 12 weeks; fully online. |

### What we will NOT say (from the brand guide)

- No job promises, placement rates, salary numbers or "get hired in 12 weeks".
- No "no coding experience needed" or "become an expert".
- No fake urgency ("last chance", "only 2 seats left") unless it is actually true.
- No student employer names unless we have permission.

**How to answer "Will this get me a job?":** Say plainly that we can't promise a job. What we can promise is real projects, mentor feedback and a portfolio you can show employers. The rest depends on the job market and your own effort.

### Message angle by group

All groups get the same core message. The opening and examples change:

- **Students:** Lead with message 1 (stand out with real projects).
- **Career-switchers:** Lead with message 2 (mentor support) and message 3 (step by step pace). Calmest tone.
- **Working professionals:** Lead with message 4 (fits around work) and the practical skills (SQL, visualisation).

## 4. Channel strategy

### Email (the only channel)

- **Why it fits:** Everyone on the list opted in. Email is low cost, we can tailor it to each group, and we can measure every step from open to enrolment.
- **Format:** Plain, mostly text emails with one clear link per email to the enrolment page. Short paragraphs. Every email ends with the standard sign-off.
- **Effort:** Low to medium. About 9 emails to write, some with 3 group versions.
- **Budget:** The main costs are the email-sending tool and staff time. See section 8.

### Low-cost extra: one live online Q&A session (optional)

A free, 45-minute online Q&A with the Lead Mentor, promoted only by email. This is still within "email only, no paid ads", because we invite people by email and host it on a free or existing video tool.

- **Why:** Career-switchers in particular may need to "meet" a mentor before they trust us. A live session lets them ask about effort and support directly.
- **Effort:** Medium (one session to plan and host, plus a recording to send afterwards).
- **Decision needed:** See section 10.

### Things we need in place (not channels, but required)

- **Enrolment page** on our website, live and tested before 7 December. Every email links here.
- **Tracking links** (links with tags that tell us which email someone clicked from), so we can see which emails lead to enrolments.
- **List split into three groups** (A, B, C). If we don't know someone's group, they get the general version.

## 9. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | What could happen | What we do |
|------|-------------------|------------|
| **Holiday timing** | Weeks 3 and 4 fall over Christmas and New Year, so people read less email and decide more slowly. | Put most emails in weeks 1 and 2. Pause sends 22–27 Dec. Consider keeping enrolment open into early January if the cohort start date allows. Alternative: move the campaign start to Monday 4 January 2027. |
| **Goal is ambitious** | 7.5% of the list enrolling is high for a paid course, and we have no past data. | Check progress weekly. If week 1 is well below 50, adjust messages based on replies rather than adding pressure. |
| **Pressure to sound urgent** | When numbers are behind, there's a temptation to write "last chance" or "only a few seats left". | Only mention a deadline or seat limit if it's true. Brand-review every email before it sends. |
| **Emails go to spam** | A large send to a list that hasn't heard from us in a while can land in spam folders. | Check when the list last received an email. Clean out bounced addresses. Keep emails plain and send from a known address. |
| **Career-switchers feel it's not for them** | The most nervous group may drop off. | E4 answers their worries calmly. The Q&A gives them a real person to ask. |
| **Enrolment page problems** | Broken links or an unclear page waste every email. | Page live and tested by 1 December. Test every link before each send. |
